## Reference 1 — Vercel Design (in-industry: agency/dev tools)

**URL:** <https://vercel.com/design>  (WebFetched 2026-05-14)
**What works:** A whole studio page built around ONE numeric idea — "247 x 56 designers, engineers, and researchers." That's the entire hero. No "we craft digital experiences" headline. The grid of team-member cards (avatar + name + Twitter + title + location) IS the page. Vercel says "the work is the people."
**One move to steal:** A signature numeric hero that's just team scale + locations, used as a confidence move. For Velkina that would be "2 operators × 8 years × Istanbul → Bucharest → Berlin" — the math of being small but specific.

## Reference 2 — Instrument (in-industry: digital agency)

**URL:** <https://www.instrument.com/>  (WebFetched 2026-05-14)
**What works:** The hero is the client logo wall + the latest project carousel — "30+ Awards with ŌURA" as the only stat. They never say "we are great"; they show the partnership. Section labels are descriptive ("Recent Work," "Services") not creative.
**One move to steal:** A "Recent Work" ledger that's literally the proof — named clients, dated, with one-line description per row. Velkina already had this in v6 (the ledger); v7 should push it to be MORE central, not a side element.

## Reference 3 — BASIC/DEPT (in-industry: digital agency)

**URL:** <https://www.basicagency.com/>  (WebFetched 2026-05-14)
**What works:** DragDragDrag — the interaction itself is the signature. Horizontal-drag carousel of work with a "Watch Reel" video as the hero. Tagline "Easy to understand, impossible to ignore." rendered with confidence. Client logos as proof: Google, KFC, Patagonia, AT&T.
**One move to steal:** A *single repeated interaction* (drag, scroll-pin, hover) used as the page's identity. For Velkina: a horizontal-drag ledger of recent work that requires the visitor to drag the row to read each project. The interaction IS the proof of craft.

## Reference 4 — Stripe Press (cross-industry: independent publishing)

**URL:** <https://press.stripe.com/>  (WebFetched 2026-05-14)
**What works:** Minimalist palette (white + black + selective bold accent — pink/yellow/blue used only on the "We Are As Gods" film section). Every book gets a dedicated landing page. The "living cover" concept — book covers link to deeper content (workbooks, interviews, PDFs). Modular grid scaling across 20+ publications without fatigue.
**One move to steal:** The dedicated-deep-page approach. Each Velkina case study is its own landing page (not a card in a grid that opens a modal). Treats projects as gateways rather than endpoints. Plus: selective bold accent applied to one section only — the same restraint signals taste.

## Reference 5 — Teenage Engineering (cross-industry: hardware / industrial design)

**URL:** <https://teenage.engineering/>  (WebFetched 2026-05-14)
**What works:** Numbered product codes (PO-12, EP-133, OB-4) as branded nomenclature. Industrial-design vocabulary translated to consumer products. Product imagery dominates; minimal narrative copy. "Quality and functional design through advanced engineering" is implied by visual restraint, not stated as a tagline.
**One move to steal:** Numbered project codes. Velkina's recent work could be VK-01 (Lavinia Bistro), VK-02 (Atar Avcı), VK-03 (TP Thermoplast), VK-04 (Rain Group), VK-05 (Nova Health), VK-06 (EduTurkia). The numbering is the visual signature — a small move that signals "operator discipline" without saying it.

## Synthesis — direction-commit input

Looking at the 5 refs, the moves I want to borrow:

1. **From Vercel Design:** a numeric hero that's brutally specific about scale ("2 operators × 8 years")
2. **From Instrument:** "Recent Work" ledger as the proof-of-existence above the fold
3. **From BASIC:** a single repeated interaction (horizontal drag) as the signature
4. **From Stripe Press:** dedicated deep pages per project + restraint on color
5. **From Teenage Engineering:** numbered project codes (VK-01 ... VK-06)

The ONE move I will NOT borrow:
- BASIC's "Watch Reel" video hero (Velkina doesn't have a reel, and faking one is exactly the kind of agency-theatre Nalba banned in v6)
- Stripe Press's "living cover" concept (too publication-shaped for a 2-person studio)
- Vercel Design's grid of 247 people (we have 2 — making the smallness LOUD is the point)
